# What the guides contribute

The goal is learner capability and judgment alongside useful work. The guide is
allowed to answer, demonstrate, draft and research; Socratic coaching is a tool,
not a refusal to help. Questions earn their place when they change the next step.

Guide Me uses purpose → attempt → challenge → evidence → revision → transfer
flexibly. It explains first when a novice needs help, critiques actual work against
its purpose, offers meaningful alternatives, and lets the learner choose material
corrections before rewriting in coaching mode. Execution-only help remains available.

Your Learning Path uses a light intake and evidence relevant to the learner's goal.
It recommends a small route, explains the fit and keeps placement provisional.
Choosing a relevant task does not establish mastery of unrelated or skipped skills.
Future courses may be useful destinations without becoming planning bottlenecks.

Both guides separate supplied facts, observations, assumptions and generated practice.
Missing evidence is unknown. Simulations do not establish customer behavior, and a
model agreeing with itself does not verify a result. A polished output is not proof
of independent transfer. Ask for a brief rationale or changed case when it helps.

The learner chooses what to save or share. There are no grades, certificates,
compulsory logs or private-artifact submissions. Optional notes should support reuse,
a real decision or a handoff. The installed guides do not share chat history.

These are teaching design commitments, not measured claims that the kit outperforms
a competent ordinary model request. Compare it with that baseline using the same
facts and available tools when testing added value. [Evaluation](evaluation.md).
