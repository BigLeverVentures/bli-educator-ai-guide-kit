# Our approach to practical AI education

[Home](https://github.com/BigLeverVentures/bli-for-educators/blob/f3a108fb0644514c4066c3ceb0c6e351300a91e8/README.md) · [Authoring kit](https://github.com/BigLeverVentures/bli-for-educators/blob/f3a108fb0644514c4066c3ceb0c6e351300a91e8/educators/authoring-kit.md)

## Begin with a useful job

Choose a situation a learner recognizes: an email they repeatedly write, a job application, a decision, a question about customers, or a small task worth automating. Name the useful result before writing the lesson. Catalog size and prompt length are not success measures.

Ask what teaching adds to an ordinary request supplied with the same facts and tools. Possible contributions include a better way to frame a problem, evidence the learner would otherwise omit, a check that catches a consequential mistake, practice responding to feedback, or a method that transfers to another task. If the contribution is thin, simplify, combine or remove the lesson.

## Conversation is the entry skill

Learners do not need a perfect prompt. They can say what they want to accomplish, explain where they are stuck and ask the assistant to guide them with questions, explanations and options. They can ask for help building a prompt and then test whether that prompt works. The learner keeps responsibility for judgment; a conversation does not guarantee a solution.

## Teach a proportionate loop

1. Understand the task, facts and desired result.
2. Inspect the proposed approach. For a small task, one sentence may be enough.
3. Do the work with the assistant.
4. Challenge the result using checks specific to the task.
5. Let the learner choose what to change, accept or verify.
6. Revise and recheck material changes; keep the useful result.

Do not turn every email into a project-management exercise. A separate reflection log, mandatory upload or worksheet must earn its burden. Usually the improved artifact holds the learning.

## Separate things that behave differently

- **Instructions:** how the assistant should help, including tone, boundaries and review behavior.
- **Source context:** the facts and documents it should use.
- **Current input:** the particular request, recipient, role or decision today.

A persistent workspace can help with sustained work. A single email needs only a conversation. Uploaded files, web access, execution and export are capabilities to verify, not powers a prompt can grant.

## Preserve judgment and evidence

A critique is another fallible output. Check important suggestions against the actual facts. Ask the assistant to wait for learner choices before replacing the work. Recheck meaningful revisions and stop when further changes no longer help.

Simulated customers are rehearsal, not evidence of demand. Invented sources, achievements, financial inputs and tool actions are not acceptable substitutes for real evidence. Check consequential claims with sources, arithmetic, actual execution or appropriate human expertise.

## Design for adults and beginners

Explain unfamiliar terms at the point of use. Say what to copy, what to replace, where to paste it and what to do after the answer arrives. Provide fictional examples when someone lacks safe material. Keep personal work with the learner; sharing is optional. Avoid grades, certificates, compulsory AI brands and arbitrary lesson durations.

Use [AI Basics](https://github.com/BigLeverVentures/bli-for-educators/blob/f3a108fb0644514c4066c3ceb0c6e351300a91e8/basics/README.md) for missing prompt, file, instruction or workspace skills. Link to the specific skill instead of making everybody complete a prerequisite course.

# Authoring kit

[Home](https://github.com/BigLeverVentures/bli-for-educators/blob/f3a108fb0644514c4066c3ceb0c6e351300a91e8/README.md) · [Evaluation](https://github.com/BigLeverVentures/bli-for-educators/blob/f3a108fb0644514c4066c3ceb0c6e351300a91e8/educators/evaluation.md)

Adapting an existing lesson? Start with [Adapt a lesson for your learners](https://github.com/BigLeverVentures/bli-for-educators/blob/f3a108fb0644514c4066c3ceb0c6e351300a91e8/educators/adapt-a-lesson.md) and its complete [volunteer-email](https://github.com/BigLeverVentures/bli-for-educators/blob/f3a108fb0644514c4066c3ceb0c6e351300a91e8/educators/volunteer-email-example.md) and [interview-practice](https://github.com/BigLeverVentures/bli-for-educators/blob/f3a108fb0644514c4066c3ceb0c6e351300a91e8/educators/interview-coach-example.md) examples. This kit is for designing or substantially reworking a lesson.

## 1. Decide whether the lesson earns its place

Write these notes for the author, not as required learner paperwork:

- Audience and recognizable situation.
- Useful result or practiced decision.
- Inputs the learner needs, plus a safe fictional alternative.
- Required capabilities and a workable fallback.
- A competent ordinary question using the same facts.
- The teaching contribution you expect beyond that question.
- How you could observe whether the contribution helped.

If the expected benefit is only a longer or more polished first answer, reconsider the lesson.

## 2. Copy this lesson outline

```text
Title: [a useful task or skill]

What you will make or practice:
[one concrete result]

What you need:
[inputs, capabilities, and a fallback]

Start:
[the learner's first action; explain unfamiliar terms]

A small example:
[demonstrate a distinction the learner needs to see]

Copy this prompt:
[complete instructions with clearly labeled changing fields]

What to do with the response:
[answer questions, inspect the approach, or choose a next step]

Check the result:
[a task-specific critique prompt or external check]

Choose what changes:
[learner selects corrections before a rewrite]

Keep and reuse:
[where to retain the approved work; try changed facts when useful]

Need a basic skill?
[link to the exact explanation, outside the copied prompt]
```

## 3. Build a purpose-specific critique

A résumé check should look for invented achievements and missing evidence. An email check should examine recipient, request, tone and commitments. A code check needs expected behavior and actual execution. Do not use a universal quota such as “find five flaws.”

Copy and adapt this critique structure:

```text
Review the work above against [purpose] and the facts I supplied.
Focus on [consequential task-specific weaknesses].
Separate supported findings from uncertainties and preferences.
For each material issue, explain the consequence and offer a correction
or a concrete way to verify it. Do not invent new requirements.
If no material issue is supported, say so.
Wait for me to choose corrections before rewriting the work.
```

This structure itself is not a tested universal solution. Adapt it to the artifact and test whether it catches the errors that matter.

## 4. Review the whole learner experience

- Can a beginner identify the first action and finish without knowing the authoring conversation?
- Does every copied prompt carry the context it needs, including any named method?
- Do field-replacement directions match actual fields?
- After following the second-case replacement directions literally, do all prompts, follow-up instructions and saved-result labels agree with the new facts?
- Are source facts, assumptions and unknowns distinct?
- Does the learner make a real correction or verification decision?
- Are claimed saves, research, execution and exports actually possible and checked?
- Does every required link work without hidden membership or paid access?
- Is optional work clearly optional? Can any reading or ceremony be removed?

Apply material fixes and review the changed experience again. After three substantial unsuccessful cycles, narrow or set aside the lesson, or identify the human/tool test needed to resolve it.

## 5. Try it, then publish an honest status

Use the [evaluation protocol](https://github.com/BigLeverVentures/bli-for-educators/blob/f3a108fb0644514c4066c3ceb0c6e351300a91e8/educators/evaluation.md). Record source review, model probes, real workflow checks, human observations and publication separately. An exact page copy proves delivery; it does not prove learning.

# Evaluation and limits

[Home](https://github.com/BigLeverVentures/bli-for-educators/blob/f3a108fb0644514c4066c3ceb0c6e351300a91e8/README.md) · [Adapt a lesson](https://github.com/BigLeverVentures/bli-for-educators/blob/f3a108fb0644514c4066c3ceb0c6e351300a91e8/educators/adapt-a-lesson.md)

## Start with a proportionate trial

For your first adaptation, follow the [small trial in the adaptation guide](https://github.com/BigLeverVentures/bli-for-educators/blob/f3a108fb0644514c4066c3ceb0c6e351300a91e8/educators/adapt-a-lesson.md). Try the complete instructions yourself with safe facts, inspect the actual result, introduce a consequential error, and try a second case. Fix and recheck material problems. Then invite a willing unfamiliar colleague to try it and record any help they need. These checks support a limited pilot, not a claim of proven learning effectiveness.

You do not need a multi-model study or our repository verification tools to teach one low-risk activity. Broader claims about superiority or portability need broader evidence. The protocol below describes how to investigate those claims; use checks appropriate to your task and the consequences of error.

## A fair comparison

Give a competent ordinary request and the lesson prompt the same substantive facts, goal and capabilities. Do not weaken the baseline to make the lesson win. Keep the evaluation criteria out of generation requests. Record extra turns, approvals and author assistance: a multi-step lesson and a one-turn answer have different costs.

Use a small set of cases before expanding:

- A normal case with enough information.
- Missing or conflicting information.
- A fresh second case to test reuse.
- An intentionally flawed artifact with known consequential defects.

Compare generated work as well as known defects. A critic that can spot a planted error may still miss mistakes in its own generated artifact. Let a learner or an explicitly identified evaluator select changes; then inspect the revision and recheck the meaningful changes.

Use more than one suitable assistant when testing portability. Record visible interface/model labels, dates, available tools, personalization conditions and any unknowns. Do not infer backend versions or equal conditions from app labels.

## Beginner probes

Give the reviewer the actual full lesson and a concrete beginner situation, without the author's backstory or expected findings. Ask it to walk through the first action, copied text, input placement, response, critique, correction choice and retained result. Keep headings, prompt boundaries and linked prerequisites intact.

A model pretending to be a beginner still has extensive knowledge. It can identify candidate gaps; it cannot establish that a human beginner can finish or benefit. Check each complaint against the real page and the fixture. Do not “fix” a missing-link complaint caused only by withholding the linked page from the reviewer.

Then observe an unfamiliar human, with consent, using their own account and safe material. Record where they stop, what help you provide, what they decide and whether they can try a changed case. Keep private work out of public evidence. This release does not claim that such human validation has been completed across the curriculum.

## Verify the actual outcome

Check source-to-claim fidelity, calculations, observed dialogue, runnable code or exported files as appropriate. A model saying “saved,” “researched” or “passed” does not prove an action happened. An author-operated test does not prove beginner usability. A second model's agreement is not independent factual verification.

## Status of this material

This is alpha teaching material. Independent human learning effectiveness and
universal provider compatibility have not been established. Try the complete
activity in your own setting before making claims about its usefulness.

## A small review record you can reuse

```text
Lesson/version and date:
Useful result and ordinary-question baseline:
Inputs/capabilities held equal:
Cases and actual checks performed:
Material findings, including failures:
Accepted changes and reasons:
Rejected findings and reasons:
Recheck results after changes:
Extra assistance/turns and unresolved limits:
Next human or workflow observation needed:
```

Keep this author-side. Do not make learners fill it in just to use a lesson.
