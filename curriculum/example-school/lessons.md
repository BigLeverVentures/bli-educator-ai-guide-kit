# Resource: email

## A clear volunteer invitation

Purpose: write an invitation using supplied facts and choose an improvement.
Inputs: a real or fictional event, audience, confirmed details and desired reply.
No browsing or paid features are required. Keep private contact information out.

For this example, Alex is inviting returning volunteers to a garden cleanup on
Saturday at 10 a.m. Bring gloves. No end time, location or reply deadline is given.

Copy this prompt into your assistant and replace Alex's facts if using your own:

> Help Alex write a short invitation to returning volunteers for a garden cleanup
> on Saturday at 10 a.m. Ask them to bring gloves. Use only these facts. Do not invent
> a location, end time or reply deadline. Put needed details outside the draft as
> questions for Alex. Explain one writing choice briefly.

After reading the draft, send this critique prompt in the same conversation:

> Check the draft against the supplied facts and its purpose. Identify any invented
> details or unclear action. Suggest useful changes, but wait for my choice before
> rewriting. If a missing fact matters, say it is unknown.

Choose which changes to accept and supply any needed facts. Ask for that revision,
then check the revised facts again. Save the useful invitation in notes you control.
For transfer, try a different invitation and explain which structure you reused.

# Resource: check

## Check a claim before sharing it

Purpose: separate a claim from support and decide whether to share or investigate.
Input: a redacted claim plus the source text available to you. Tools: browsing is
optional; without it, work from pasted text and mark external verification undone.

Fictional claim: “All volunteers prefer Saturday mornings.” Supplied evidence:
two volunteers said they can attend this Saturday. This does not establish what all
volunteers prefer or whether those two generally prefer mornings.

Copy this prompt and replace the fictional claim and evidence if desired:

> Compare this claim with its evidence: “All volunteers prefer Saturday mornings.”
> Two volunteers said they can attend this Saturday. Separate what is supported,
> what is unknown and what would need another source or question. Do not treat
> missing evidence as proof of the opposite. Ask me to choose whether to revise,
> investigate or omit the claim before writing a replacement.

Choose a response and explain the most important reason. Check that a revised
claim says no more than the evidence. For transfer, examine a different claim from
another context with the same distinction. This is supported practice, not a grade.
